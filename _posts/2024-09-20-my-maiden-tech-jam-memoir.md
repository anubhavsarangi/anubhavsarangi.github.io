Mood: Feeling refreshed and confident, yet humbled by the journey ahead.

*A journey through presentations, nerves, and public speaking…*

### **Chapter 1: The Unexpected Call**

I know, I know… You’re probably wondering why am I suddenly dropping this unusual message here on a Friday evening. Is this going to be one of those messages, yikes!? Well, stick with me—it’s a bit more than that.

It’s the story of how I recently found myself navigating something a bit out of my comfort zone.

It all started when Santosh casually reached out earlier this year: *‘Hey, HZ recommended us to deliver the Tech Jam.’* 

Immediately, I was excited but, let’s be honest, also terrified. You know that feeling when your stomach is full of butterflies? Yeah, mine weren’t the happy, graceful kind; they were more like the chaotic, nervous kind, the ones that fly in circles and don’t know how to land. But the journey started from there, and we slowly began piecing everything together. And just like that, I was pulled into what I can only describe as Project: DataHub API Deckitecture—a journey that ended up feeling almost as long as building the DataHub API itself—seriously, that’s how long it took me to put this thing together! But trust me, this wasn’t just another project. This was my first real deep dive into the world of public speaking, and it wasn’t exactly a cakewalk.

### **Chapter 2: The Presentation Rollercoaster**

Let’s talk about presentations for a second. We’ve all been there, right? You start with a couple of bullet points, thinking, “This will be easy.” I started with basic bullet points, feeling confident at first, but then it hit me—this needs to look good. Cue the deep dive into Google for inspiration. Amazon presentations, Apple keynotes… and that’s when I realized just how far off my slides were from looking remotely presentable.

So, I went back to the drawing board, pulling images from Freepik and icons from Flaticon, my trusty buddies for image assets. Stitching it all together, I thought, “Hey, this looks kinda cool now!” But then, the inevitable happened. I revisited the Apple keynotes and thought, “Okay, how do I make my presentation look that cool?” There’s always a constant urge to improve—whether it’s making the slides visually appealing or refining how you deliver your message. It’s a journey, for sure. Today felt like my first baby steps into the world of public speaking—something none of us are really taught in school but absolutely need in the real world.

They say communication is key, and boy, have I realized just how true that is. We’re constantly bombarded with information—technical sessions, best practices, architectural patterns, you name it. But at the end of the day, none of it sticks unless it’s communicated well. That’s what drives impact. It’s crucial to present these ideas in a way that not only makes sense to both technical and non-technical folks but also explains the “what,” “why,” and “how” behind it. Why does it matter to the business? Why should they care? 

*Sigh.* That’s what I learned in today’s Tech Jam—well, that, and a whole lot more.

### **Chapter 3: The Grind**

We’d been prepping for this for months, ever since we first built that initial slide deck. And looking back now, compared to what we have today, let’s just say that first draft was… well, a bit of a disaster. Let’s call it ‘**Slide Deck 1.0: Not-So-Final Edition**’—because believe me, it wasn’t even close.

For seasoned pros—PMs, solution architects—these kinds of presentations are no big deal. They prep the night before and ace it like it’s nothing. But for me? It was another project altogether, running parallel to building the DataHub API. I guess you could call it **Project: Deckitecture**—because it felt like I was architecting an entirely new system just to get those slides in shape.

### **Chapter 4: The Delays and New Dates**

Originally, the Tech Jam was scheduled for August 13. When the invite first landed in my inbox a month before, I stared at it, counting down the days like a ticking clock. The nerves set in—my presentation wasn’t ready, and I had no idea what I was going to say. My solution? I binged TED talks—*Julian Treasure’s “How to Speak So That People Want to Listen”,* *Amy Cuddy’s “Your Body Language May Shape Who You Are”*. Despite this, I still felt like a deer in headlights. But as luck would have it, fate intervened—by the end of July, I got the glorious news that the presentation had been pushed to September 19, thanks to room availability issues during the Annual Awards event. I sighed in relief. Procrastination had just been granted a month-long extension.

August slipped away, and it was suddenly mid-September, and the butterflies were back—this time a little more hopeful but equally jittery. On September 16, just days before the presentation, I received a clashing invite for a celebratory lunch for the mewatch Paris Olympics team. Of course, it had to happen the same week! After some coordination, we moved the presentation to September 20. It was a win—another day’s buffer. Oh, and let’s not forget my in-laws and extended family arriving on the 19th. I knew that somewhere in my subconscious, I wouldn’t be able to fully enjoy their company until this Tech Jam was over.

### **Chapter 5: Friday Arrived—The Big Day**

Friday arrived—the big day. My alarm went off at 6 AM (nothing new there). I hopped on the MRT and reached the office at 8:10. The first thing I did? Booked a meeting room for three hours to rehearse. Time for the dry run.

My aim: fit 56 slides into a sharp 30-minute presentation.

By 9:15, I wrapped up the first round—it took 46 minutes. But I knew I had to cut it down to 30 minutes to leave space for Santosh, who had his 10-15 minute part. I needed to hit that internal optimize button—you know, the one that kicks in when you need to speed up without rushing. I ran through it again and at around 9:50, Santosh joined me with some golden advice, especially on how to explain complex charts and diagrams. His suggestion to write out brief pointers made a huge difference. Writing down the explanations helped imprint the content in my mind, so it came out naturally when I spoke without having to think on my feet. After two more practice runs, we were feeling good by 11 AM. I wouldn’t say *completely* ready, but good enough.

Santosh then got a call from Nat, the MC of Tech Jam, and we headed to the Atrium to set up and do mic/speaker tests. With legs that felt like jelly, I took a quick break to calm my nerves. A scroll through some random news headlines, a few deep breaths, some water, and a bit of stretching later, I was ready to get back to it. A little trick I picked up from those TED talks—channel the adrenaline into something useful.

### **Chapter 6: Showtime**

At 11:35, I headed back to the stage area. The audience started to pour in. The last five minutes felt like a countdown in my mind: tick tock, tick tock. When 12 PM finally hit, it felt like a church bell rang twelve times inside my chest.

Nat introduced Santosh, who kicked things off. As I waited, my hands grew cold, as if I were standing at base camp, though we were very much at sea level. When Santosh handed me the mic, then came my turn. *For a split second,* I froze—like, is this really happening? *I had this intro all planned:*

*“I’m Anubhav Sarangi, and I like to think of myself as a bit of a metadata magician. You know, the kind of person who spends their day making sense of a million moving data points so the rest of us can enjoy clean dashboards and seamless analytics.  Most days, I’m wrangling data, optimizing python codes, and ensuring our analytics flow like a perfectly brewed cup of coffee… strong and reliable.”*

And yeah…. that didn’t happen. I completely skipped over my intro and jumped straight into the presentation. In hindsight, I wonder if people even caught my name!

Despite the shaky start, I quickly found my rhythm. Slide by slide, I was in the flow. There were a few pauses—thanks to remote controller issues—but I used those moments to gather my thoughts and, giving the audience time to process what I had just said. As the slides progressed, I could feel the nerves melting away. Somewhere inside, a new confidence was brewing. It was actually going better than I had anticipated.

By the time I reached the final slides, I was in the zone and transitioned smoothly back to Santosh, wrapped it up with a punch. And then, the floor opened for Q&A.

The audience response was fantastic! People were clapping, and there were cheers for our team. I felt validated when Anil Nair had questions about data loss scenarios and how to recover missing data. Santosh explained how we handle the segregation of the APIs and how we could recover those lost numbers from AWS. The audience really connected with the error capture and analysis—especially when we showed the error rates.

Haizhou also chimed in with valuable insights on proactive monitoring. It looks like we’ll be suiting up for the next challenge soon!

Once the Q&A wrapped up, it was time for the moment everyone had been waiting for: the free lunch! Anil, HZ, and others approached me with kudos for the presentation. Anmol from CNA said it was intuitive and easy to follow, even for non-technical folks. Alvin from the SEO team commented that our work, though highly technical, was presented in a way that made sense to a broader audience.

Santosh and I exchanged a cheerful high-five, celebrating our teamwork before heading to lunch with Nat. Lunch was a welcome break from the technical grind. Nat joked about how long we’d been prepping compared to other teams, and we all laughed—yes, it was definitely longer than usual, but it paid off! He also shared some amusing stories about his yearly trips to Switzerland and how he’s become bored of Geneva. It was the perfect way to unwind after weeks of prep. We laughed, we relaxed—it was *Friyay* indeed.

### **Chapter 7: The Final Stretch of the Day**

By the time lunch wrapped up, it was 4 PM, and I had to rush home for the next big event—hosting dinner for my in-laws and extended family. It was like going from *deck-building* to *dinner-building* in a flash. One minute, I’m talking about architecture diagrams, and the next, I’m making sure everyone’s plates are full. It was one of those days where you’re handling a big work project and then hosting family, all in one go. Exhausted? Yes. But at the end of it, there’s a sense of satisfaction that makes it all worth it.

### **Chapter 8: If Only There Were AI for That…**

Speaking of making life easier, wouldn’t it be great if we had an AI that could handle all this for us? Now, here’s the thing—if only there were some kind of brain-mapping AI that could instantly translate my thoughts into speech. Seriously, just imagine! No more scrambling for words, no more lost trains of thought. Just pure, seamless delivery.

AI that trains my train of thought—speaking everything perfectly, straight from the neurons. Forget a teleprompter—I need my brain wired directly to my mouth! Until then, I’ll stick to good old-fashioned nerves and some quick improvisation. And yes, maybe a script with some catchy lines like, *“Remember when everyone was comparing Intel vs AMD? Well, now it’s ARM taking the crown—and it’s here to stay.”* But hey, who’s counting?

### **Chapter 6: The Long Road Ahead**

So yeah, this is just the beginning. Today wasn’t just about a presentation—it was about learning. It was about stepping out of my comfort zone and figuring out how to share technical details with a crowd in a way that sticks. There’s still a long way to go—and, I’m ready for what’s next.

P.S. *To everyone who’s ever Googled “how to make presentations cooler,” keep going. And if you figure it out, let me know.*
